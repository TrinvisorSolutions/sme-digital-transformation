# As-Is Process Analysis

## Purpose

The As-Is analysis documents how the organisation currently performs key activities before any transformation is designed.

The objective is to understand the real process, including manual work, delays, duplicate effort, hand-offs and control weaknesses.

For this case study, the initial focus is the **customer onboarding process**.

## Current Customer Onboarding Process

### Step 1: Customer Enquiry

A customer contacts the organisation through email, telephone, website enquiry or referral.

The enquiry may be recorded differently depending on which employee receives it.

### Step 2: Initial Customer Details Captured

The employee manually records customer details in a spreadsheet or local document.

Information may include:

* Customer name
* Company name
* Contact details
* Product or service interest
* Source of enquiry
* Notes

There is no consistent validation process to check whether the customer already exists.

### Step 3: Internal Communication

The enquiry is forwarded to the relevant team by email.

Different employees may maintain their own copies of the customer information.

This creates multiple versions of the same record.

### Step 4: Qualification

The Sales team reviews the enquiry and determines whether further follow-up is required.

Qualification information is often stored in email conversations or personal spreadsheets.

There is no consistent definition of lead status.

### Step 5: Customer Information Updated

As the relationship develops, additional information is manually added to spreadsheets or other systems.

Some teams may maintain separate customer records.

### Step 6: Finance Setup

If the customer proceeds, Finance receives information by email and creates the required customer record within the financial system.

Customer details may need to be entered again.

### Step 7: Operational Handover

Relevant information is sent to Operations or Customer Service.

This may involve another email, spreadsheet update or manual document.

There is no automated confirmation that all required information has been received.

### Step 8: Reporting

Management reporting is produced by combining information from several sources.

Employees manually reconcile spreadsheets, finance records and customer information before preparing reports.

## Current Process Flow

**Customer Enquiry → Manual Data Capture → Email Handover → Sales Qualification → Manual Updates → Finance Setup → Operational Handover → Manual Reporting**

## Key Pain Points

### Duplicate Data Entry

Customer information is entered multiple times across different systems and spreadsheets.

This increases administrative effort and creates opportunities for errors.

### Inconsistent Records

Different teams may hold different versions of the same customer information.

Employees may not know which source contains the most accurate record.

### Manual Hand-offs

Information is transferred between teams through email and spreadsheets.

This creates delays and makes it difficult to track ownership.

### Limited Process Visibility

There is no single workflow showing where a customer is within the onboarding process.

Management therefore has limited visibility of delays or bottlenecks.

### Reporting Effort

Management reporting requires information to be collected and reconciled manually.

This reduces the timeliness of reporting and increases the risk of inconsistent figures.

### Weak Data Controls

There is limited validation when customer information is created or updated.

Duplicate records and incomplete information may therefore remain undetected.

## Risks in the Current Process

* Customer information may be lost or overlooked
* Duplicate records may be created
* Customers may experience delayed responses
* Employees may work from outdated information
* Reporting may contain inconsistent figures
* Manual processing may become increasingly difficult as the organisation grows
* Responsibility for individual process stages may be unclear

## Initial Improvement Opportunities

Potential improvement areas include:

* Introduce a central customer record
* Standardise customer onboarding
* Introduce mandatory data fields
* Automate hand-offs between departments
* Define clear process ownership
* Introduce customer status tracking
* Reduce duplicate data entry
* Create automated or semi-automated management reporting
* Introduce data validation rules
* Define common KPI definitions

These opportunities will require further analysis before a specific technology solution is selected.
