# Pain Point Analysis

## Purpose

The purpose of this analysis is to assess the main operational pain points identified during discovery and determine which issues should receive the greatest attention.

Not every problem has the same impact. Prioritisation helps focus transformation activity on issues that create the greatest business value, operational risk or customer impact.

## Assessment Criteria

Each pain point is considered against four factors:

* Business impact
* Frequency
* Customer or user impact
* Risk if unresolved

## Critical Pain Points

### 1. Inconsistent Customer Information

Different departments may hold different versions of the same customer record.

This creates uncertainty about which information is correct and can affect customer service, reporting and operational decision-making.

**Why it matters**

* Increases risk of incorrect decisions
* Creates avoidable rework
* Reduces confidence in data
* Can negatively affect customer experience

## 2. No Consistent Customer Onboarding Process

Customer onboarding depends heavily on individual working practices, spreadsheets and email.

There is no single standard workflow.

**Why it matters**

* Creates inconsistent customer experiences
* Makes delays difficult to identify
* Reduces accountability
* Increases the risk of missed information
* Limits scalability

## 3. Limited Management Visibility

Management reporting depends on manually collecting and reconciling information from multiple sources.

This means decision-makers may not have timely access to reliable information.

**Why it matters**

* Slows decision-making
* Makes performance problems harder to identify
* Reduces confidence in reporting
* Makes proactive management more difficult

## Root Causes Identified

Initial analysis suggests that several pain points share common root causes:

### Fragmented Systems

Customer and operational information is stored across multiple tools and spreadsheets.

### Lack of Standardised Processes

Teams perform similar activities differently.

### Limited Data Governance

There are insufficient controls around data ownership, validation and consistency.

### Manual Workflow Design

Many activities depend on email, spreadsheets and individual follow-up.

### Limited Integration

Systems do not consistently exchange information.

### Unclear Ownership

Some stages of the customer journey do not have clearly defined accountability.

## Prioritisation Recommendation

The first phase of transformation should prioritise:

1. Establishing a consistent customer data structure
2. Standardising the customer onboarding process
3. Improving management reporting and KPI definitions
4. Reducing duplicate data entry
5. Improving cross-team workflow visibility
6. Introducing automation where the process is already clearly defined

Automation should not be introduced simply to accelerate an inefficient process.

Processes should first be reviewed, simplified and standardised before automation is applied.

## Consulting Observation

Several problems initially appear to be technology issues, but the analysis indicates that the underlying challenges also involve process design, data governance, ownership and working practices.

A technology implementation alone is therefore unlikely to resolve the organisation's problems.

The transformation approach should combine:

* Process improvement
* Data improvement
* Technology
* Governance
* Change management
* User adoption
