# Discovery Questions

## Purpose

The discovery phase is used to understand how the organisation currently operates, where problems occur, what stakeholders need and what evidence is available before defining a solution.

The objective is to avoid making assumptions and to ensure that recommendations are based on business needs, user experience and operational evidence.

## Executive and Strategic Questions

* What are the organisation's main business objectives over the next 12 to 24 months?
* Which operational problems are currently limiting growth or performance?
* Which areas of the business create the greatest management concern?
* What improvements would senior leadership consider most valuable?
* What information does management currently lack when making decisions?
* Are there specific targets for efficiency, cost reduction, customer service or revenue growth?
* What level of investment is available for transformation?
* What risks or constraints could affect the programme?

## Process Questions

* Which business processes rely most heavily on spreadsheets, email or manual intervention?
* Which processes generate the most delays or rework?
* Where does information need to be entered more than once?
* Which tasks are repetitive?
* Which processes vary between departments or employees?
* Where do hand-offs between teams regularly fail or create delays?
* Which processes are difficult to measure?
* Where are approvals required?
* Which activities could potentially be simplified or automated?

## Customer Questions

* How are new customer enquiries received and recorded?
* How is customer information maintained?
* Where is customer history stored?
* How are customer complaints or service issues tracked?
* How quickly can employees access complete customer information?
* Are customers required to provide the same information more than once?
* Where do customer journeys experience delays?
* What customer experience measures are currently tracked?

## Data Questions

* What systems currently store customer, sales, operational and financial information?
* Who owns each major source of data?
* How frequently is data updated?
* Are there known duplicate records?
* Are common fields defined consistently across departments?
* How are errors identified and corrected?
* Which spreadsheets are considered business critical?
* How is data validated?
* Which reports require manual reconciliation?
* Are there concerns relating to data protection, access or retention?

## CRM Questions

* Is there currently a CRM system?
* If so, which teams use it?
* What information is captured?
* Which teams maintain separate customer records outside the CRM?
* What prevents employees from using the current CRM consistently?
* Which customer interactions should be tracked?
* What workflows should a future CRM support?
* What reporting should management be able to obtain from the CRM?
* Which systems would need to integrate with it?

## Reporting and KPI Questions

* What reports are currently produced?
* Who produces them?
* How long do they take to prepare?
* How frequently are they produced?
* Which reports require manual data collection?
* Which KPIs are most important to leadership?
* Are different teams using different definitions for the same KPI?
* Which decisions are currently delayed because information is unavailable?
* What level of reporting should be available in real time?

## Technology Questions

* What business systems are currently in use?
* Which systems integrate with one another?
* Where are technical limitations affecting business processes?
* Are there known security or access issues?
* Are there existing automation tools available?
* What technical skills are available internally?
* Which systems are considered critical to business operations?
* Are there planned system changes or upgrades?

## People and Change Questions

* Which teams will be most affected by the transformation?
* Which employees are likely to support the changes?
* Where could resistance occur?
* What concerns might employees have?
* What training is currently provided for business systems?
* How comfortable are teams with adopting new technology?
* Are there existing change champions within the organisation?
* What support would users need during implementation?

## Delivery Questions

* Are there fixed deadlines or business events that could affect implementation?
* Which teams have capacity to participate in workshops and testing?
* Are external suppliers likely to be involved?
* What dependencies could affect delivery?
* Are there budget, resource or procurement constraints?
* How should progress be reported?
* Who has final decision-making authority?
* What would determine whether the transformation is considered successful?

## Evidence to Request

In addition to interviews and workshops, the following evidence should be requested where available:

* Existing process documentation
* Organisation charts
* Current system inventory
* Sample management reports
* Customer onboarding documents
* CRM exports
* Spreadsheet templates
* Data dictionaries
* Complaint or service logs
* SLA reports
* Training materials
* Existing KPIs
* Previous improvement initiatives
* Audit findings
* Relevant policies and procedures

## Discovery Outcome

At the end of discovery, the consultant should have enough evidence to:

* Define the current-state operating environment
* Identify the most significant pain points
* Understand stakeholder needs
* Assess data and technology limitations
* Identify transformation opportunities
* Define business requirements
* Prioritise areas for further analysis
